# Carry your project into the next AI conversation

Status: draft reusable material for review. This is a brief and starter instruction, not a running AI service, a validated tutor or a guarantee that an AI system will follow instructions correctly. It works independently of the TrailGuide device.

## 1. A learner-owned project brief

Keep only what is useful. Leave fields blank when unknown. Use fictional or sanitized examples where needed; do not include confidential material, credentials, participant information or private team transcripts. Check the chosen AI service's data rules before sharing.

```text
Project: What I want to make, investigate or improve.
Why it matters: Who or what this is for.
Next useful piece: A small observable result, not the entire finished project.
Learning interest: What I would like to understand or become able to do.
Current state: What exists; what I have tried; what actually happened.
Constraints: Tools, access needs, time, budget, privacy and boundaries.
Help wanted now: Explain / brainstorm / research / collaborate / demonstrate /
                 implement / debug / critique / organize / another approach.
Decisions and evidence: What I chose, what I checked and what remains uncertain.
Next step: Something I can try or a question I need help answering.
```

The finished artifact does not have to contain AI. Someone can use AI to learn about and build an ordinary circuit, document, investigation or program.

## 2. Optional AI-coaching starter

Paste the relevant brief above and adapt this instruction for the current task:

```text
Help me make progress and learn through this project. Use my goal and the next
useful piece as the focus; do not replace it with an unrelated course.

Use Define -> Direct -> Check -> Adjust as a light working loop. If essential
context is missing, ask one useful question; otherwise help me try a bounded step.

Ask what kind of help I want when it is unclear. Offer explanations, options,
hints, examples, demonstrations, collaboration or direct implementation as
appropriate. Do not force hints, withhold access support or turn the conversation
into an unrequested test. If I request implementation, help implement and verify;
if I want to practice a part myself, leave room for that.

Treat AI roles as choices for this step, not a ladder or a score. A non-AI route,
a human specialist or stopping can be appropriate. Do not assume more AI use is
better.

State assumptions and uncertainty. Explain choices concisely. Distinguish source
claims from inference; use original sources for research where possible. Never
invent citations, test results or successful execution. Suggest checks proportionate
to the consequences and make clear what I can inspect myself.

Respect my scope, privacy and tool permissions. Do not act on external systems,
change hardware or publish material merely because we are discussing a project.
Use sanitized examples instead of requesting confidential data or credentials.

After a useful step, offer a short optional reflection: what happened, what I now
understand, and what I want to try next. At a stopping point, offer an updated
portable brief with decisions, evidence, open questions and a next step, so I can
continue without depending on this conversation or its facilitator.
```

## 3. How a mentor can use it

- Ask the learner which part of their project matters now, rather than filling in their goals for them.
- Help create one useful context brief; let the learner own and edit it.
- Try one small step in the learner's preferred help style and inspect what happened.
- Ask whether the brief is enough to continue, and what help they would still want.
- Keep asking for human expertise legitimate. Independence does not mean abandoning support.

No submission, tracking, profile, assessment or device history is required. Do not upload the brief to the public repository. Review usefulness with voluntary feedback; the prompt's existence does not establish reliable coaching behavior or learning outcomes.

See [the broader mentoring direction](COACHING-DIRECTION.md) and the existing [AI Learning Lab](https://github.com/luminerdy/AILearningLab). These materials adapt its project practice, not a competing curriculum.
